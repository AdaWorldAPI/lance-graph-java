THE FINDING
  Under a distribution where dispatch actually costs, the split architecture wins ~4.7x --
  because the selector layer (classid -> ClassView -> mask) creates the information ONCE,
  before the sweep, where the monolithic generic loop re-derives it per row and eats the
  mispredict every time.

  AND THE LAWFUL SHAPE IS NOT THE COMPROMISE. E' was expected to pay for obeying the
  mask-native law (masks are the sanctioned currency; D''s index list is a materialized
  population, forbidden as internal currency). Measured, it costs 2-3% in the sweep and
  WINS decisively on the step that actually differentiates them:

      partition scan, Java scalar loop over the classid column : 41.01 ms
      mask build, ndarray::simd::eq_u32_strided_to_mask, 1 call:  2.49 ms   (16.5x faster)

  Amortization is the whole game, and the mask changes its terms:
      B' per pass 0.3131 ms; E' per pass 0.0671 ms; saving 0.2460 ms/pass
      break-even for the 2.49 ms mask build :  ~10 passes
      break-even for the 41.01 ms Java scan : ~167 passes
  The lawful currency is 16x cheaper to CREATE and pays for itself 16x sooner. Obeying the
  law is the fast path, not a tax on it.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW
  - NOT "Java is faster than Rust". The winning kernels ARE Rust; the masks are built by
    Rust (ndarray::simd). A Rust-alone program that built masks first would match E'. The
    win comes from specialization PLACEMENT, not language.
  - What IS architectural: the knowledge lives in the classid/ClassView layer, the
    population stays a mask, and the sweep is a bulk call over it. That is the mask-native
    execution shape the workspace law already specifies -- now with a measured reason.
  - Part 1 is the control that keeps part 2 honest: when dispatch is predictable,
    partitioning buys NOTHING (D <= B). Entropy reduction pays exactly when there is entropy.
